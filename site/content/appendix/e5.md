## Setup

30 agents on a complete graph, tempered selection over T ∈ [0.05, 2] (14 values), κ ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6}, ε = 1.
Sources: constant, moving with mean dwell 1000, 100 or 10 rounds, or a new random source every round. 3000 rounds,
3 repeats, GPU. Script: `python -m nichenet.experiments.e5`. Data: `data/site/e5.json`; final networks and their
formation for the map previews: `e5_structures.json`, `e5_evolution/`.

A run's regime is set by two quantities:

- **commitment:** mean over agents of the largest share of their attention, in the final network;
- **tracking:** attention on the current source, averaged over the second half of the run.

A run is *spread out* if commitment is below ½, a *coordinated hierarchy* if it is committed and tracking is at least
half the commitment, and *lock-in* (over-betting) if it is committed but tracks less than that.

## Results

Majority regime of three repeats:

- **Constant source:** coordinated hierarchy below T½ and spread out above, for every κ. The boundary falls exactly at
  T½: 0.29, 0.43, 0.57 and 0.71 for κ = 0, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.6.
- **Any moving source with κ > 0:** below T = κ the group locks in. The symmetric state is unstable there, attention
  condenses on whichever agent is ahead, and after the first move that is a stale one. Between κ and T½ a hierarchy
  forms that follows the source; above T½ attention is spread. The window has width (1 − κ) ln ρ / ln(N − 2); it
  narrows as the source moves more often and is gone when it moves every round.
- **κ = 0 never locks in,** because nothing reinforces the leader.
