## Setup

30 agents on a complete graph, tempered selection at T = 0.3. The true source moves with mean dwell τ_E; the news lands on
it with probability 1 − ν = 0.5 and on a random agent otherwise. Runs last 40 τ_E; 15 memories, 4 repeats, on the GPU.
Memory is varied in two ways: through ε (with κ = 0), τ_m = 1/ln(1 + ε), and through κ (with ε = 1).

Runs are scored by log-loss on the true source, mean over listeners of −ln A[s, i] ([chapter 2](/measures/#benefit)).

Script: `python -m nichenet.experiments.e1 [eps|kappa]`. Data: `data/site/e1.json`, `e1_kappa.json`.

## Results

| τ_E | 10 | 30 | 100 | 300 | 1000 | 3000 | slope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| τ_m* with ε as memory (κ = 0) | 1.93 | 3.08 | 5.04 | 8.16 | 13.7 | 23.3 | 0.43 (local 0.42 → 0.49) |
| τ_m* with κ as memory (ε = 1) | 6.19 | 8.09 | 9.31 | 11.6 | 13.9 | 15.3 | 0.16 |

- **ε behaves as a memory.** The best τ_m grows with τ_E, with an exponent that approaches ½ from below, the scaling of
  single learners in noisy niches.
- **κ behaves as commitment.** As κ approaches T the fixed point condenses onto the source (u* = ρ^{(1−κ)/(T−κ)},
  [chapter 3](/regimes/#boundaries)): log-loss falls to 0.02 against 1.4 for ε, and the optimum barely moves with τ_E.
- Noise is what makes memory valuable: with a perfect signal the shortest memory always wins.
